C&P+ Limitations

Species requiring an unassessable character

In C&P+, couplets involving the upperside, forewing spaces 2–3, or genital characters offer a Cannot determine option. For most species this makes no difference — enough underside characters remain to rank them uniquely. But 12 species rely on at least one of these skippable couplets as their sole distinguishing step. When those couplets are skipped, they tie with a sibling species rather than reaching a unique rank&#nbsp;#1.

97 / 109 species reach rank #1 from underside characters alone

These are genuine taxonomic limitations of the C&P key, not tool errors. The key itself states that certain pairs require the male upperside or a genitalia dissection to separate. C&P+ is transparent about this: when the decisive character cannot be observed, the two candidates share the top rank.

Why this happens. These species pairs are separated in the C&P key by a couplet that requires observing the male upperside, measuring forewing spaces 2–3, or examining genital structure. C&P+ marks those couplets with a Cannot determine option rather than forcing an answer. When the option is used, the scoring system treats the couplet as neutral and both candidates remain tied. The affected pairs are genuinely indistinguishable from underside photographs alone — the tool is reflecting reality, not failing to identify them.
